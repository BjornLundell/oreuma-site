Starting again

A workout app without streaks, and why that helps if you've fallen off before

Oreuma has no streaks, on purpose. Streaks help some people, but if you've stopped exercising before, the broken streak is often the exact moment you stop opening the app. A comeback needs an app that welcomes you back.

By Bjørn Lundell, founder of Oreuma · 10 October 2026

What streaks do well

Streaks are good at one thing: making an unbroken run feel valuable. If you already exercise regularly and enjoy keeping the chain going, they can be fun, and there's nothing wrong with that. If a streak works for you, keep it.

What happens when the streak breaks

The trouble starts at the first gap, and gaps always come. A 40-day streak that resets to zero turns a normal week off into a visible loss. Psychologists describe a related pattern, sometimes called the what-the-hell effect: once a rule is broken, people often abandon it completely instead of getting back on track.

For someone who has quit exercise before, that moment is familiar. The app that felt motivating in week two now greets you with a reminder of what you didn't do. Many people quietly delete it, and the streak that was meant to build a habit ends up ending it.

What to look for instead

  • No streak counter or missed-day score. Your history should show what you did, never what you missed.
  • A gentler session after a break. The app should notice you've been away and make coming back easy, without a penalty.
  • Short sessions that count. A 10-minute workout on a low day should count as a workout.
  • Reminders that don't scold. If it nudges you at all, the message should be about today, not about the days you missed.
  • Little to set up. No account and no long questionnaire means less friction on the day you finally open it again.

How Oreuma does it

Oreuma has no streaks, no account and no email signup. Each day it builds a bodyweight session of roughly 10 to 30 minutes, sized to how long it's been since your last workout. After a month or more away it halves the usual volume and leaves out jumping. Every finished workout counts as progress, and sessions grow harder only as you keep completing them.

If you've been away a while, Oreuma sends at most two gentle reminders, and neither mentions missed days.

If you have a heart or lung condition, are pregnant or recently gave birth, or are coming back from an illness, injury or surgery, check with your doctor or a physiotherapist before you start. Stop if something hurts sharply, and get help if you feel chest pain, faintness or unusual breathlessness.
